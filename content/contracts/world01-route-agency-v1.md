# World 01 route agency cross-check v1 — synthetic authoring only

This is a **spoiler-free, offline** consistency check for the public first-world mechanical skeleton (`world01-opening-skeleton.json`) and its synthetic causal outline (`world01-causal-authoring-v1.json`). It is not a runtime validator, private-world review, story storage mechanism, production authorization, or import gate.

`validateWorld01RouteAgency(world, causal)` requires two distinct committed exits from one choice scene, exactly matching that scene's outgoing options. Each branch must reach a shared convergence without visiting the other branch beforehand, and every pre-convergence continuation must have a path to that reunion. The two branches' reachable pre-convergence scene sets must be disjoint: if they share an intermediate scene, the claimed reunion happens too late and validation rejects it as `premature_convergence`. Shared scenes after the declared reunion remain valid. However, reunion-side links must not reach the committed choice or any pre-reunion branch scene (even indirectly via the opening): such links could replay a different route commit and overwrite the NPC/route consequence ledger. This fails with `route_reentry`. Revisits wholly inside the post-reunion region remain valid. A reachable cycle **before** convergence is rejected with `route_cycle`, even when an optional exit exists: reachability alone cannot guarantee that a player will ever leave a loop. This strict first-world authoring check does not forbid post-convergence revisits or establish a general rule against free exploration loops in other worlds. The two committed events need unique identities and route traces. Each event must produce its own NPC relationship state, and the post-convergence outcome mapping must preserve those differences and the committed event history.


The opening is identified by a unique `orientation` node (as in the first-world skeleton), or by a unique zero-incoming-edge root when no orientation is declared. Every node must be reachable from that opening. The committed choice must dominate the branch entries, shared reunion, and endings: an opening-to-branch/reunion/terminal bypass or a pre-choice cycle fails closed with `choice_bypass`. Ambiguous openings fail with `entry_invalid`; disconnected nodes with `graph_unreachable`. A valid acyclic approach to the choice and post-reunion revisits, including a return to the opening, remain legal when the opening is explicitly marked. These are strict first-world mechanical authoring checks, not a restriction on other worlds' exploration structures.

Undeclared top-level world or causal-outline keys, as well as keys in route commits, causes, effects, NPC continuity, convergence, route outcomes, or learning settings, are rejected rather than silently accepted. The top-level allowlists cover the existing public mechanical skeleton and synthetic causal fixture; they do not inspect private content or certify the values of optional descriptive fields. Convergence may preserve only the three reviewed route-memory fields. The required public projection must use committed state, keep claims unverified without authorized discovery, and explicitly prohibit exposing internal IDs or uncommitted consequences. These are authoring-side declarations, not a runtime security boundary.

The synthetic NPC baseline must identify one actor present and available at the committed choice, with a neutral relationship and unverified claims. Claim promotion must remain explicitly tied to authorized fact discovery. A route-commit reaction may set **only** the NPC relationship; extra effect fields (including availability or knowledge changes) fail closed instead of being silently accepted. This checks an authoring declaration, not actual event authentication or NPC persistence.

Scene nodes accept only the reviewed structural fields `id`, `next`, and optional `role`, `learning`, `worldMutation`. Undeclared per-scene fields fail as `graph_invalid`. If a mutation mode is declared, it must be `none` or `on_commit_only`; automatic/on-entry mutation claims fail as `node_mutation_invalid`. Optional learning labels must be safe identifiers, not explicit forced/required/mandatory/auto-complete markers; a route scene omitting `learning` or declaring `learning: none` contradicts the required optional opportunity and fails as `learning_route_gap`. These are author-side consistency checks only: metadata cannot establish actual server behavior or prove a lesson is optional at runtime.

Optional learning must be offered in **both** route scenes, with explicit player consent and a nonacademic continuation. This does not mean the player must complete a practice or that the academic work has been verified. The author-side check also rejects fabricated measurements and narrator-committed effects. All errors are fixed codes without IDs or text; input is not mutated.

Run `node --test test/world01-route-agency.test.mjs` under Node 22. The 109 synthetic tests cover successful and reversed-route registration, collapsed choices, mismatched exits, invalid commits, cross-route bleed, missing convergence, self-loops and multi-scene pre-convergence cycles, legal acyclic detours and post-convergence revisits, opening-choice bypasses and pre-choice cycles, disconnected scenes, premature shared scenes, legal distinct detours and post-reunion sharing, NPC state erasure, incorrect consequence attribution, lost route memory, one-sided learning and forced academic gates. This is a **separate cross-check**, not a replacement for the existing world01 causal lint, DM/NPC bridge, scene-learning validation, real Worker/D1 tests or model experience review.

Published `world.acceptance` examples, when present, are executable mechanical assertions rather than unchecked route prose. Exactly two simple branch routes must start at the declared opening, follow actual directed edges through the committed choice and declared reunion, cover the two different committed exits without crossing branches, and end at a terminal scene. Stale paths, repeated scenes, missing/duplicate branches, unreviewed fields on route examples, and malformed acceptance arrays fail closed with the fixed `acceptance_invalid` code. Non-route acceptance statements remain descriptive, not verified runtime behavior. This check is optional for small synthetic fixtures without an acceptance section and does not validate private narrative content.

Optional public metadata is also fail-closed: warning strings must be bounded plain text; `ownership` must declare ChatGPT as narrative author and Claude as engineering owner; `authoringConstraints` must be a bounded, unique list of nonempty strings; and `sceneContract`, when present, must be a local JSON contract path. For `world01`, the scene contract pointer must match `content/contracts/world01-opening-skeleton.json`. Invalid metadata returns only `metadata_invalid`. This validates shape and linkage, **not** the truth, safety or secrecy of arbitrary prose; private content must never be placed in these public fields.

Non-route acceptance entries are descriptive but no longer an untyped escape hatch: each must have exactly `id` and a bounded `require` string, all acceptance IDs must be unique across route and descriptive entries, and unknown fields fail with `acceptance_invalid`. The check does not claim to execute the descriptive requirements or verify runtime behavior.

A world with no finite completion route from its declared reunion fails as `ending_unreachable`, even when all nodes are otherwise reachable from the opening. A terminal reunion is allowed in minimal synthetic graphs. Post-reunion cycles and optional revisits remain legal when at least one reachable terminal exit exists. This is graph-level authoring evidence, not proof that the runtime will select or execute an ending.

The first formal world remains **DO-NOT-IMPORT**. No private plot, character identity, hidden fact, answer, approved dialogue, or production state belongs in this contract or its tests. Real per-field private review and Catherine's explicit authorization are required before any official-world import.
